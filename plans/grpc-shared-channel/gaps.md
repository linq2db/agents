### Aggregate

4 of 5 findings trace to blocks a Tier L plan would have required: `P4` (SUG001), `P8` (MIN001 and SUG002) and the step-4 convention embed (NIT001). NIT002 traces to a rule that does not exist yet. The dominant class is `GAP-05` (2 of 5): the tests exercise the new behaviour but cannot go red on the effect the PR claims, which is disposal of the context's own channel and a server-side rollback. Both rules that would have caught this are already in `work-plan.md:152` and `:166`. They only bind through a plan, and this branch had none, so the highest-yield single change is to carry the "name the deletion mutation that reddens it" requirement into the no-plan path (the test rubric and `test-writer`). Otherwise the spread is flat: one assumption about a dependency, one embedded convention, one missing provider-scope rule.

### Recommended durable fixes

- GAP-05 × 2 → a rubric rule in `docs/review-rubric/rubric-tests.md` item 6 (and the same line in `agents/test-writer.md`) — sharpen "every new public member needs at least one test exercising it" to "…one test that goes red when the member's body is removed, observing the member's own effect (the disposed resource, the rolled-back row) rather than a guard upstream of it; a claim of rollback needs a statement executed before the cancellation point". This is the no-plan counterpart of `work-plan.md:152`/`:166`.
- GAP-02 × 1 → an attack vector in `plan-critic.md` (or a `P4` rule in `work-plan.md`) — any user-facing statement about a dependency's ownership or disposal semantics (what a channel, client or pool disposes, and under which option) needs a `P4` row probed against the dependency's source predicate, enumerating every input that predicate reads, not only the one the doc names.
- GAP-01 × 1 → the semantics of `P8` `providers:` in `work-plan.md:144`, mirrored in `testing.md` → *Test layout* — scope must be justified in both directions: an obligation whose axis is provider-independent (transport, lifetime, cancellation mapping) names one configuration and says why, and a provider family needs a reason why its members can disagree.
